My name is **Antonio Blescia** and I live in Bari, Italy. I have always worked on two things
that may seem far apart: **cyber security** and **research on assistive technology for people
with disabilities**. What ties them together is my many years of experience in **industrial
automation**.

## My background

I have worked for years in *industrial & automation*. That is where I learned to make software,
electronics and machines work together, and to design systems that must always run reliably and
safely, even when something goes wrong. On that foundation I follow two paths side by side.

### Cyber security

I work on the defensive side of security: **incident response**, **threat hunting**, malware
analysis and **digital forensics** (DFIR). In practice, I look for the traces an attack leaves on
a system, reconstruct what happened and help stop it.

- I contribute to open source security tools. For example, I published the
  [Windows.Audit.CISCat_Lite](https://docs.velociraptor.app/exchange/artifacts/pages/windows.audit.ciscat_lite/)
  artifact on the Velociraptor Artifact Exchange.
- I have presented my research at the **ConfSec** conference.
- With [DFIR Chronicles](https://ablescia.github.io/DFIR-Chronicles/) I tell real attack
  techniques, and how they get caught, as comics.

### Assistive technology

In 2013 I published **ThinkUino**, an open source project that connected an Arduino board to an
EEG headset: you could switch a device on just by concentrating. In 2014, in Bari, I started
[SmartChair](/en/research/smartchair/), an electric wheelchair controlled by brain waves,
together with home automation. Today, twelve years later, I am picking that work up again and
extending it to other aids, such as augmentative and alternative communication with
[Kite](/en/research/kite/).

The two paths often meet. An aid that moves a person, or speaks for them, is a connected device,
and it has to be **safe** in both senses: it must not cause harm, and it must not be possible to
tamper with it. Back in 2014, among SmartChair's limits, I already listed data security as an
open problem.

## Why I do it

I have specific technical skills in automation, electronics, software and security. I believe
that having these skills also brings a **responsibility**. That is why I feel it is my duty to
use them to improve assistive devices for people with disabilities, which are often expensive,
hard to adapt and closed.

You can also find me on [GitHub](https://github.com/ablescia) and
[LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/antonio-blescia-2a495a19a/).

## Why I do research in the open

> We break down the barriers of movement through open technology, making autonomy
> and scientific sharing accessible to everyone.

I believe that the knowledge needed to give someone back their autonomy should be a
**common good**. That is why I share the work while I am doing it, not only at the end.

## How I work

1. **Open hardware.** Schematics, PCBs, 3D models and bills of materials are published
   under open hardware licenses.
2. **Free software.** The code is public, documented and reusable.
3. **Shared data.** When participants consent, I publish anonymised datasets following the
   [FAIR](https://www.go-fair.org/fair-principles/) principles.
4. **A transparent journal.** Every step goes into the [journal](/en/journal/), including
   the attempts that did not work.
5. **Participatory design.** *Nothing about us without us*: the people who will use these
   aids take part in designing them.

## Safety

This is a **research journal**. The prototypes described here **are not certified medical devices**
and must not be used without the supervision of qualified staff. I also publish the known
limits and risks of every component.
