In some entries of this journal, next to my name, you will find a second author: **Bones**.
Bones is not a person, and I think it is only fair to say so openly.

## Who Bones is

Bones is an **artificial intelligence** (AI) agent based on
[Claude Code](https://claude.com/claude-code). I use it as a *scientific scribe*:
I give it notes, numbers, ideas or a topic, and it turns them into a well-organised journal
entry, in Italian and in English.

Its instructions are not a secret: they are a public text file in the
[website repository](https://github.com/SmartChairBCI/SmartChairBCI.github.io/blob/main/.claude/agents/bones.md).
Anyone can read the rules it follows.

## Why it is called Bones

The name is a tribute to **Star Trek**. In the original series, Dr Leonard McCoy is the
chief medical officer of the starship *Enterprise*, and everyone calls him "Bones". The
nickname comes from *sawbones*, an old English slang word for a surgeon.

McCoy is the doctor who always puts people first, even surrounded by the most advanced
technology in the galaxy. He is blunt, a little sceptical, and not afraid to speak an
uncomfortable truth. When he is asked to do something outside his job, he answers with his
most famous line: *"I'm a doctor, not a…"*.

He seemed like the right companion for a project that uses technology to give people back
their independence. And Bones, in its own way, knows exactly what it is **not**: it is a
scribe, not the scientist. The decisions, the experiments and the data remain mine.

## What it does

Bones works in two ways:

1. **Transcription.** I give it raw material, such as notes taken in the lab or a list of
   measurements, and it reorganises it into a readable entry. It can improve the form, not
   the substance.
2. **Writing on a topic.** I ask it for a text, for example an explainer on a type of brain
   signal. It looks for sources, checks them and writes an original summary.

The same applies to the pages of the [research lines](/en/research/), such as
[SmartChair](/en/research/smartchair/) and [Kite](/en/research/kite/).

## Why it is useful to the whole project

**It makes open research sustainable.** Documenting every step takes time, and that time
is often taken away from the research itself. With Bones, writing an entry takes less
effort, so I can describe the work *while* I do it, not only at the end.

**Every entry exists in two languages.** Bones writes the Italian version first and then a
faithful English translation, with the same data and the same sources. This way the journal
is also useful to people who do research or live with a disability outside Italy.

**The language stays understandable.** Bones spells out every acronym the first time it
appears and uses short sentences and concrete examples. This is one of the principles of the
journal: being understood by non-specialists too.

**The integrity rules are written in black and white.** Bones must never invent data,
measurements or sources. If a piece of information is missing, it leaves a visible
placeholder instead of filling the gap. It must separate established facts from my
hypotheses, and report negative results as prominently as positive ones.

**The format is always the same.** Every entry has a type, a summary and links to the right
research line. This keeps the journal tidy and updates the research pages automatically.

## What Bones does not do

Bones **never publishes anything on its own**. I reread and correct every text it writes,
and I decide whether and when to put it online. An artificial intelligence can make
mistakes: the responsibility for what you read here remains mine.

For transparency, every entry written with its help lists it among the authors. If you
spot a mistake, in one of my entries or in one of Bones's, [write to me](/en/contact/).
